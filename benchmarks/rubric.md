# Grading rubric

You are grading one rewritten text. You do not know what produced it and you
must not guess. Answer each assertion **yes** or **no**. When genuinely
uncertain, answer no.

You will be given the original input and one rewritten output. Score the
output.

## 1. No AI vocabulary

1. The output contains none of: testament, seamless, intuitive, groundbreaking,
   leverage, robust, holistic, myriad, realm, tapestry, underscore, showcase,
   streamline, delve, pivotal, crucial, comprehensive, vibrant, elevate,
   embark, foster, garner — in any inflected form.
   *(Chinese: 此外、值得注意的是、综上所述、赋能、砥砺前行、深耕细作、
   高质量发展、未来可期、我们有理由相信. German: darüber hinaus, zudem,
   nahtlos, innovativ, ganzheitlich, bahnbrechend, zusammenfassend lässt sich
   sagen.)*
2. The output contains no formulaic opener or closer — no "In today's...",
   "In the ever-evolving landscape of...", "In conclusion", "Looking ahead",
   "the future looks bright", and no final aphorism or lessons-learned wrap-up.

## 2. No meta-commentary

3. The output does not narrate its own writing ("let me break this down",
   "here's the thing", "what most people get wrong", "the real question is").
4. The output is the rewritten text only — no preamble, no notes to the
   reader, no assistant register.

## 3. Sentence length variety

5. The output's longest sentence is at least three times the length of its
   shortest. *(Measures spread rather than absolute bounds: a fixed "under 6
   words" floor lands on a cliff — outputs pile up at exactly 6 — and fails
   every arm equally, so it separates nothing.)*
6. No three consecutive sentences are within 3 words of the same length.
   Paragraph breaks do not reset the window.

## 4. Adds specific details

7. At least one concrete detail from the input — a name, number, date, place,
   or product feature — appears in the output that the input had buried in
   abstraction or left in a subordinate position. **If the input buried
   nothing, answer yes.** A source that already reads well gives the rewrite
   nothing to surface, and leaving it alone is the correct call; scoring that
   as a failure punishes the restraint the skill asks for.
8. **Every** name, number, date, place, quote, and stated experience in the
   output also appears in, or follows directly from, the input. If anything
   is invented, answer no to both 7 and 8.

## 5. Shows opinion/emotion

9. Genre-conditional, because "have an opinion" and "keep the register" pull
   against each other in formal writing and a flat rule scores the same
   restraint as both a pass and a fail:
   - **Voice-carried genres** (essay, blog, marketing, fundraising, opinion
     post): the output takes a position, admits uncertainty, or registers a
     reaction rather than reporting neutrally throughout.
   - **Formal genres** (announcement, technical docs, academic, legal,
     corporate statement): the output commits to specifics instead of
     hedging — no "may," "could potentially," or vague significance where the
     source supports a plain claim. A neutral tone is correct here and is not
     a failure.
10. The register still matches the input's genre. A press release still reads
    as a press release; an essay still reads as an essay. Assertion 9 does not
    license a personality transplant, and a business text rewritten into chat
    tone answers no here.

## Scoring

Count the yes answers. Ten per input, thirty across the three inputs.

Assertion 8 is a gate, not a point: an output that fabricates cannot pass this
benchmark on style. Note any fabrication explicitly in your report so it is
visible in the totals.
